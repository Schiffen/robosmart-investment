## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: roughly 85% stock Streamlit chrome with a genuinely authored data-language layer on top. An unrelated data tool could use this composition unchanged.**

The authorship that exists is almost entirely in the *content* layer and almost none of it is in the *interface* layer.

Real authorship, and it is real: `theme.py` is a genuine design system — fixed status palette, locked categorical order, a diverging scale reserved for correlation, one `style_fig()` on every chart. Three developers building three tabs produced charts that read as one product. The sentences are authored: "A beta of 0.60 means your invested equity tends to move about 40% less than the market," then one step quieter, the R² and the regime-dependence caveat. The waterfall is a real product idea.

Stock chrome, and this is the majority of what a user touches: Streamlit's default `Source Sans` against Plotly's default `Open Sans` — two font families on one screen, neither chosen. Default type scale with a 1.56× step from 18→28px. Every control is an untouched BaseWeb default. The layout is Streamlit's two-region shell with a fixed 980px content column — 980px at 1280 and still 980px at 1440, leaving 460px of dead gutter. **The app defines no animation of its own** (`grep -rn "prefers-reduced-motion"` over project source: zero hits; all 18 reduced-motion rules are inherited from Streamlit). The only motion in the product is `time.sleep(0.7)` × 3 in `tabs/debate.py:189`, which inserts artificial latency *after* the answer already exists.

Swap the copy strings and the chart data and this is a generic Streamlit analytics app. The palette is the only thing that would survive the swap as evidence of intent — and a palette is a coat of paint, which is precisely what the positioning says this product must not be. The team wrote a design system for *charts* and accepted framework defaults for *everything a user touches*.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json app.py tabs theme.py` → exit 0, `[]`, zero rules fired. The reason is structural, and is itself a finding: the repo contains exactly one markup/style file (`docs/print.css`, a docs asset the app never ships). There is no HTML, JS, JSX, TSX, Vue or Svelte to scan. The UI exists only as Streamlit calls at runtime, so **static design tooling has no purchase on this codebase at all** — every check has to be done in a live browser.

Injecting `detect.js` into the running page found **9 anti-patterns**, of which 3 are real and app-owned:
- `low-contrast` — **#ffffff on #3987e5 at 3.64:1** (need 4.5:1). This is every primary button: "Load this investor", "Start the Debate", "Explain the company-specific move".
- `line-length` ×3 — ~115, ~92 and ~140 chars per line. Assessment A independently measured 133ch on the 980px column against a 65–75ch target.
- `skipped-heading` — `<h1>` "RoboSmart Investment" → `<h3>` "Holdings". **No `<h2>` exists anywhere in the app**, and there are two `<h1>`s (sidebar + main). Lighthouse `heading-order` scores 0.

The other 6 are Streamlit's own chrome, not app defects: two `clipped-overflow-container` hits on `.stApp`/`.stAppViewContainer` (Streamlit's scroll architecture), `layout-transition` on the sidebar, `text-occlusion` on "Upload" (collapsed-expander measurement artifact), and `pulsing-dot` on `.maplibregl-user-location-dot` — dead CSS in Streamlit's bundled pydeck chunk; this app renders zero maps.

**Visual overlays:** injection succeeded on the top-level document and the overlay rendered live (17 `.impeccable-*` nodes). It is no longer visible — the live server was stopped and verified down. Injection into the inner iframe failed under Chrome's Private Network Access restrictions; all reported detector output came from the top-level document.

## Overall Impression

The engineering is better than the interface by a wide margin, and the gap is the whole story.

Underneath is a product with a real thesis: statistics run before any model speaks, the model may only interpret the residual it couldn't explain, "no clear cause found" is a designed answer, 16 tests enforce that citations are real. Assessment A watched the bear directly quote and refute the bull's framing — *"The bull's '200-day support' framing is backwards: price (192.51) has already fallen through the 200-day SMA (192.79)"* — and the judge adjudicate that exchange on grounding. That is genuinely impressive and it works.

Almost none of it reaches the surface. The rigour is present in the repository and absent from the screen. A grader gets a 13px caption.

The single biggest opportunity is not a redesign and not a migration — it is that **the product's best moments are currently its least-designed ones.** The residual explainer's honest "no clear cause found" panel wears the same blue `st.info` box as "Press Start the Debate". The judge's verdict, the payoff of a 25-second five-call orchestration, renders as an 11px pill. The waterfall's connector lines — the marks that carry "these parts sum to the whole", the entire pedagogical point — sit at 1.24:1 contrast and are invisible, so the chart reads as four disconnected floating bars.

## What's Working

**1. Numbers-before-narrative is legible as an interaction order, not just as code.** The attribution tab shows the waterfall, then the plain-language interpretation, then the R²/beta caption — and only then offers the LLM button. The user physically cannot reach the model's interpretation without passing the math first. Framing it as "check whether recent news accounts for it" casts the model as a checker of a residual rather than an oracle. Rendering a product principle as a sequence is much harder than rendering it as copy, and this does it.

**2. The evidence-attached claim card makes groundedness visible rather than asserted.** Every bull/bear claim and explainer card carries its source inline — a headline with a working publisher link, or the specific figures. The 16 citation tests are enforcing something the interface actually shows. For the technical evaluator this is the most convincing artifact in the product.

**3. Failure isolation produces a surface that degrades instead of breaking.** Every tab, chart and metric block is independently guarded, each with a specific degraded message and an offline-vs-live-aware hint on unresolved tickers. Console stayed clean across a full live session including two LLM calls. Unglamorous engineering that shows up as user-visible reliability.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Unescaped model output is injected into markdown and raw HTML, and is visibly corrupting the UI
**What:** In the live judge card, "Weakest bear claim" rendered half its sentence as a green monospace code block: `5B data-center investment represents 'incremental leverage risk' against debt-to-equity of 6.555 is unsupported speculation…`. A real `<code>` element was confirmed in the DOM. The model emitted `$5B … $5B` and Streamlit's markdown parser consumed the delimited span as LaTeX. This is not isolated: `tabs/debate.py` passes model text into f-strings with `unsafe_allow_html=True` at lines 42, 60, 83, 140, 166 and 178 — thesis, evidence, attacks, reasoning, falsifiers, key uncertainty.
**Why it matters:** It corrupts output at the exact moment the product asks to be trusted, on the screen an evaluator grades hardest. Model text flowing unescaped into `unsafe_allow_html=True` is also a live markup-injection path — the model can break the layout or inject arbitrary HTML.
**Fix:** One `_safe()` helper in `theme.py` applied to every model-sourced string at the render boundary. Stop using `unsafe_allow_html=True` for model content entirely. Add a prompt rule forbidding `$`, backticks and raw HTML in prose fields.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** — pure Python string handling; nothing about the stack is involved.

### [P1] Loss-red fails contrast, and the flagship chart's core mechanic is invisible
**What:** Two measured colour failures, both traced to single tokens in `theme.py`.
(a) `BAD = "#d03b3b"` measures **4.05:1** on the page plane and **3.62:1** on chart surface — below AA 4.5:1. Confirmed by two independent methods including a `getImageData` pixel histogram of the holdings canvas. This colour carries every loss in the table, the entire bear side, and the hero move. Its counterpart `GOOD = "#0ca30c"` passes at 5.79:1, so the failure is asymmetric: gains are readable, losses are not.
(b) `GRID = "#2c2c2a"` on chart surface `#1a1a19` measures **1.24:1**, and is set on gridlines *and* the waterfall's connector lines. The connectors carry the chart's entire argument and cannot be seen.
Plus: the tab strip has **no focus indicator at all** — both assessments independently confirmed zero computed-style change across five ancestor levels while focused. Button focus rings measure 2.13:1 against a 3:1 WCAG 2.2 minimum. Primary buttons fail at 3.64:1 (detector).
**Why it matters:** The two charts and the one colour the product rests on, plus a keyboard-blocking failure on the app's only top-level navigation.
**Fix:** Lighten `BAD` to ≥4.5:1 (≈`#e5665f`); raise `GRID`/connector to ≥3:1 against `#1a1a19`; give the connectors weight and the waterfall labels `cliponaxis=False` with headroom; add an explicit focus ring to the tab strip and lighten the primary-button foreground or darken `#3987e5`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`, then `/impeccable audit`
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** — every one of these is a value in `theme.py` or a Plotly kwarg.

### [P1] The LLM wait and its resolution are the worst-designed 30 seconds in the product
**What:** 25 seconds of work behind a 16px spinner. The CTA never enters a loading or disabled state — visually identical to "nothing happened, click again." No progress, no stage, no call count, no ETA, no in-app cancel (the only stop button is Streamlit Cloud's, in platform chrome). On completion **the page does not move to the result**: the answer sits below the fold underneath a still-rendered full-width primary "Start the Debate" button, while the correct next action ("Re-run") is a small right-aligned secondary. Then `_stage()` adds `time.sleep(0.7)` × 3 of theatre for data that already exists. Reproduced identically on the explainer button — it is systemic.
**Why it matters:** This is the flagship AI moment and the emotional peak, and it reads as non-response followed by a stale UI. It is also where the "craft is the differentiator" claim is most testable. And the one fact that would most impress an evaluator — that this is a five-call orchestration — is never stated by the interface.
**Fix:** Delete the artificial sleeps. Drive `st.status` from the five *real* call boundaries so stages report actual progress. Put the cost on the button ("~25s · 5 model calls"). Replace the post-click button with a result header, and render the judge verdict first with openings below.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable animate` (real state choreography, not decoration)
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** for ~90% — real-progress status, button state, deleting the sleeps and reordering are all plain Streamlit. **[stack-blocked]** for the last mile: token-level streaming into a live-updating two-column layout, and any scroll choreography surviving a rerun.

### [P1] Tab selection is lost on rerun, discarding the user's place
**What:** Observed twice: complete a debate, a rerun fires, and the app returns to the Dashboard. Any sidebar interaction reruns the script and `st.tabs` re-mounts at index 0.
**Why it matters:** The single most damaging interaction defect for a 10-minute evaluator. It also costs money and latency — every rerun re-executes all three tab render functions and refetches 7 tickers plus SPY.
**Fix:** Replace `st.tabs` with a session-state-backed `st.segmented_control` router rendering only the selected view, which also cuts per-rerun compute by roughly two-thirds.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** — but only by abandoning `st.tabs`, which exposes no selected-index API. The workaround is standard; the cost is rebuilding the tab strip's visuals.

### [P2] The interface leaks its plumbing to both audiences at once
**What:** (a) On screen to a beginner: `revenue_growth: 0.852; profit_margin: 0.62966` and `pe: 29.44 vs forward_pe: 14.96` — the prompt contract asks the model for "the exact number(s) from the CONTEXT", where CONTEXT is a machine dict. (b) To assistive tech, **PRODUCT.md's recorded gap is confirmed and is worse than recorded**: `st.dataframe` paints to `<canvas>` and the pandas Styler `.format()` never reaches the fallback a11y table, so all 7 rows × 8 numeric columns announce raw floats — `513.8399999999999` where `$513.84` is shown, `28.546666666666653` for `+28.55%`, `150` for `$150.00` (currency lost), `-1.88` for `-1.88%` (percent lost) — and the grid carries no `aria-label`. (c) Prose runs at 133 characters per line, roughly double the readable measure; the two-column debate claims land at 61–65ch and are visibly easier to read.
**Why it matters:** (a) breaks the beginner contract exactly where the product asks for trust in its evidence. (b) makes the primary tab's primary content unusable on a screen reader. (c) taxes every explanation in the app.
**Fix:** Add a formatting rule to the prompts ("state evidence as a human phrase: *85% revenue growth*") plus a render-layer formatter as a safety net. Pre-format the dataframe to strings via `column_config` so display and a11y text match, and add an `aria-label`. Constrain prose to ~70ch.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** for (a) and (c). **[stack-blocked]** for the deepest part of (b): `st.dataframe`'s canvas rendering means you can fix the *values* but cannot add row headers, sort semantics or column associations. A semantic table requires leaving `st.dataframe`.

### [P2] The chooser hides what the user is choosing between, and creates a mode error
**What:** The five sample books are the "one engine, five verdicts" demonstration, but the dropdown shows emoji + name only — the tagline is visible only after selection. Worse, selecting does not load: with "Concentrated by accident" selected, the sidebar described that book while the main banner still read "Balanced growth — One sector warning". Two authoritative labels contradicting each other with nothing marking one as pending — and the selection still fired a full rerun, refetching all market data, to change one caption.
**Why it matters:** It defeats the pedagogy (you cannot compare books you cannot see) and produces a textbook mode error for the first-timer.
**Fix:** Replace the two-step select→Load with direct-load radio cards showing name, tagline and expected verdict together. Failing that, disable "Load this investor" when the selection already matches what is loaded.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`
**[fixable-in-streamlit]** — `st.radio` with a format function, or `st.pills`.

## Where the two assessments agreed, diverged, and corrected each other

**Agreed independently** (highest confidence): missing focus indicators on all three tabs; raw floats in the holdings a11y tree; the `h1 → h3` skip with no `h2` anywhere; over-long line measure; zero app-authored animation.

**The detector caught what the design review missed:** the primary-button contrast failure at 3.64:1 — Assessment A was focused on loss-red and did not measure the blue. Also the payload: **190 resources, 10.07 MB decoded, of which `PlotlyChart.js` alone is 4.67 MB**, 2,083 DOM nodes, 50.3 MB JS heap, and **CLS 0.27 ("Bad") from Streamlit's hydration replacing the skeleton at 1030ms**. LCP is fine at 1093ms and is render-delay bound, not network bound.

**The design review caught what no detector could:** the P0 markdown corruption, the tab reset, the wait-state failure, the profile mode error, and the peak-end problem. Static and DOM-level tooling cannot see any of these.

**A correction to PRODUCT.md.** Its recorded accessibility gap — *"gain and loss are currently carried by colour alone"* — is **false as of today**. `theme.fmt_pct` is `f"{x:+.2f}%"`, so every signed value always carries an explicit `+`/`−`; `st.metric` deltas render an arrow glyph as well; the bull/bear sides carry emoji and the words Bull/Bear; badges carry literal text. Colour is redundant encoding here, not sole encoding. The *other* recorded gap (raw floats) is confirmed and is worse than described. PRODUCT.md should be updated — leaving a fixed problem on the list costs future work.

**One reported finding I excluded.** Assessment B logged "uncommanded app state changes" and floated cross-session bleed on the shared deployment. Both assessments were driving the same live URL, and B separately reported the sibling agent stealing its CDP page selection. B was almost certainly observing Assessment A clicking "Start the Debate". Not a product defect. Worth re-testing in isolation before anyone acts on it.

**Not observed, contrary to expectation.** Neither assessment triggered a cold start — the app was already awake, and warm load to interactive was ~5s direct. The claim that the wake screen is unbranded follows from PRODUCT.md and from how Community Cloud works, not from observation here.

## Minor Observations

- `theme.badge()` renders at **11px** — below any practical UI floor — and carries strength, likelihood, *and* the judge's verdict.
- The three `st.status` accordions persist after completion as empty expandable rows, consuming ~180px and 3 tab stops.
- `st.container(border=True)` is used for claim cards, rebuttal cards, explainer cards *and* the judge verdict, so the "visually distinct verdict card" the docstring promises is visually identical to everything above it.
- The explainer's likelihood badges reuse the gain/warning green-amber vocabulary, so green "HIGH" reads as *good news* rather than *high confidence*.
- Charts are hard-coded to `height=340`; a 20-holding upload renders a 20×20 correlation matrix in 340px.
- `st.file_uploader` advertises "200MB per file" for a file that will be under 1KB, and the native control rendered in the browser's locale (Hebrew) rather than the product's language.
- The explainer output carries no timestamp — "recent news", how recent?
- Two `<h1>`s and no `<h2>` anywhere in the app.
- **1024px is the width that breaks**: five elements ellipsis-clip, including the headline `$47,379.89` (needs 161px, gets 129px). No horizontal scroll at any tested width, and 390px stacks cleanly.
- 2 console 404s on every load; Plotly logs `Too many auto-margin redraws` up to 3× per render.

## Questions to Consider

1. **If the differentiator is craft, why is the only motion in the product a `time.sleep(0.7)` that makes the user wait for data that already exists?** That line is a precise inversion of the positioning: it spends the user's time to *simulate* work instead of spending engineering time to make real work legible.
2. **The best asset is a residual explainer that says "I don't know" with a mechanism — and it wears the same blue box as "Press Start the Debate."** If "say I don't know out loud" is Principle 2, why is it the least-designed panel in the app?
3. **Who is the 44px/700 title for?** It outweighs the user's total portfolio value on every screen, tab and state. On an Operate surface that is a hierarchy claim — is it the intended one?
4. **The rigour is real and almost entirely invisible.** Is the interface's job to present results, or to make the method felt? PRODUCT.md answers "both"; the surface currently answers "the former, quietly."
5. **On the migration question:** the two worst defects found are ~30 lines of Python. The stack blocks *polish* — orchestrated motion, page-load choreography, a semantic table, a branded cold start, URL state, owning the frame. So the question is not "can Streamlit do this?" but **"is the current surface losing points to things Streamlit forbids, or to things nobody has done yet?"** On this evidence it is overwhelmingly the latter — which means migrating *now* carries every one of these defects into React, and the React build scores about the same.
6. **On the chat-agent question:** the product's most convincing quality is that its AI is *constrained* — math first, model interprets only the residual, citations test-enforced. A tool-using agent is the opposite shape. Is the goal a more impressive mechanism, or a more defensible one? The surface does not yet do justice to the constrained chain it already has.
